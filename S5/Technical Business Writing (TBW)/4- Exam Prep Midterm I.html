1Ethics, Culture & Legal Case Studies

Complete, forensic solutions to recurring FAST Midterm I exam scenarios

FAST Midterm I exams place heavy weight on case study analysis. These scenarios test your ability to apply Pfeiffer's ethical principles, Edward T. Hall's cultural framework, and organizational communication ethics to realistic workplace conflicts.

Case Study 1: The CEO's Deceptive Blog Post on Layoffs
Scenario Prompt: The CEO's Blog Post During Impending Reductions

Company sales have steadily declined, cost-cutting is visible, and informal conversations are dominated by speculation about layoffs. Amid this uncertainty, a post from the CEO appears on the company's internal blog:

"With news of workforce adjustments elsewhere in our industry, we realize many of you are concerned about the possibility here. I'd like to reassure all of you that we remain confident in the company's fundamental business strategy, and the executive team is examining all facets of company operations to ensure our continued financial strength."

Employees interpreted this message as an indication that layoffs were unlikely. However, one month later, the CEO announced a workforce reduction affecting 20% of the company (700 employees). Employees learned that senior leadership had already been discussing large-scale layoffs when the blog post was published, but information was withheld to avoid panic and protect investor confidence.

Questions:
1. From the perspective of writing ethics and professional communication, evaluate whether the message can be considered ethical. Give reasons.
2. What ethical responsibilities do organizational leaders have when communicating with employees during uncertainty?

Forensic Model Solution: Comprehensive Ethical Evaluation

1. Ethical Evaluation:
No, the CEO's message cannot be considered ethical. Even though it may not contain a direct, literal lie, it was deliberately written to create a false impression of job security, which directly breaches the standards of ethical professional communication.

• Misleading Wording & False Reassurance: Phrases like "I'd like to reassure all of you" sound comforting but intentionally skirt the truth. Employees reasonably concluded layoffs were unlikely.
• Suppression of Material Information: The CEO concealed the most critical fact: that senior leadership was already actively discussing large-scale job cuts.
• Principles Violated (Pfeiffer Ch. 1):
  • Honesty: Intentional deception through ambiguity.
  • Avoiding Harm: Employees were denied the critical window to save money, curb expenses, or look for alternative employment in a weak job market.
  • Fairness: Investors' financial comfort was prioritized at the direct expense of employees' livelihoods.
  • Respect for Confidentiality: While leaders must protect confidential deliberations, confidentiality justifies silence (e.g., "We cannot comment on restructuring at this time"); it never justifies affirmative misleading reassurance.

2. Ethical Responsibilities of Leaders During Uncertainty:
• Be Truthful: Avoid half-truths, euphemisms, or vague comforting generalities.
• Share Timely Information: Disclose what can be shared as early as possible so affected stakeholders can plan.
• Be Transparent About What Cannot Be Said: State clearly that operations are under review and provide an exact date for the next update.
• Avoid Emotional Manipulation: Influence employees with verified facts and evidence, not false emotional solace.

Case Study 2: Ahmer's PhD Patent vs. CureMD Dilemma
Scenario Prompt: Student Patent Work at New Job

Ahmer completed his Ph.D. developing an IT-related solution to a medical problem. He joins CureMD as a senior developer. At CureMD, he discovers that the software problem his team is struggling with can be solved using the exact algorithms from his Ph.D. research report, which is currently undergoing the formal patent licensing process with his university supervisor.

If Ahmer reveals what he knows, he will be an immediate hero at CureMD. However, doing so could compromise the privacy rules of the patent process and deny his original university royalties.

Questions:
1. What are the relevant ethical considerations and legal issues in this dilemma?
2. What course of action should Ahmer take?

Forensic Model Solution: Intellectual Property & Conflict of Interest

1. Relevant Ethical & Legal Considerations:
• Intellectual Property & Patent Secrecy: Patent law requires absolute novelty. Disclosing unpublished patent details to a commercial third party prior to grant can invalidate the patent application entirely.
• Conflict of Interest & Royalty Rights: The research was co-developed with his academic supervisor and funded by the university. The university holds intellectual property rights and expected financial royalties.
• Respect for Confidentiality (Pfeiffer Ch. 1): Ahmer is legally and ethically bound by his academic Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA).

2. Recommended Course of Action:
• Ahmer must not unilaterally hand over the code or disclose the proprietary patent mechanisms to CureMD.
• Instead, he should inform CureMD management that a patented, state-of-the-art solution exists and initiate a formal corporate technology licensing agreement between CureMD and his university's Technology Transfer Office (TTO).
• This legal bridge makes him a hero at CureMD, protects the patent, and legally secures royalties for his university and supervisor.

Case Study 3: Asma's Remote Freelance Client Decision
Scenario Prompt: Remote Freelancing Across US, Singapore, and Australia

Asma, a fresh Computer Science graduate from Pakistan, receives remote offers from three clients:
• US Client (California - UTC-8): Fast-moving startup; values low-context, direct, concise updates via Slack and Zoom.
• Singapore Client (UTC+8): Mid-sized tech firm; high-context style, indirect feedback, relationship-oriented, email and occasional calls.
• Australian Client (UTC+10/11): Small business; informal, friendly, time-conscious, straightforward communication.

Questions:
1. How does working remotely change the way Asma should communicate with each client? Draw a comparison.
2. Which client might be easiest to manage given her location in Pakistan (UTC+5)? How should she set boundaries?
3. Besides money, discuss two key factors Asma should consider before deciding.

Forensic Model Solution: Cultural Context & Time-Zone Management

1. Remote Communication Differences:
• US Client (Low-Context / Direct): Must provide explicit, data-driven updates (e.g., "Auth module is 80% complete; tests conclude Thursday 3 PM"). Fast-paced, concise, task-focused.
• Singapore Client (High-Context / Indirect): Requires careful interpretation of indirect feedback. If they say "Perhaps we could examine this module again," it signals polite dissatisfaction. Must invest in relationship-building and follow up calls with written confirmation.
• Australian Client (Low-Context / Conversational): Friendly, egalitarian, and straightforward, but highly time-conscious. Deadlines and technical milestones must be clear and respected.

2. Managing Time-Zones from Pakistan (UTC+5):
• Easiest to Manage: Singapore (UTC+8). Singapore is only 3 hours ahead of Pakistan. A normal working day in Pakistan (9:00 AM – 5:00 PM) overlaps with Singapore (12:00 PM – 8:00 PM), allowing regular synchronous communication without midnight shifts.
• Boundary Setting: Asma must establish clear working hours, agree on asynchronous response SLAs (e.g., 4-hour window), and avoid answering non-urgent pings after hours to prevent burnout.

3. Non-Financial Factors to Consider:
• Long-Term Career & Skill Growth: Which project offers cutting-edge technologies, mentorship, and portfolio-worthy deliverables?
• Cultural & Communication Fit: Whether she prefers explicit, rapid task assignments (US) or relationship-driven stability (Singapore).

Key Takeaways

  • Deceptive communication is unethical even if technically free of direct lies.
  • Confidentiality warrants silence or transparent deferral, never false comfort.
  • High-context cultures require reading between the lines; low-context cultures require explicit, concise facts.
  • Time-zone overlaps determine synchronous vs. asynchronous feasibility in global software work.

2Tone Drills & "You" Attitude Rewrites

Transforming negative, accusatory, and writer-centered messages

FAST exams frequently provide poorly written, aggressive, or selfish paragraphs and require students to rewrite them into polished, reader-centered correspondence.

1. High-Yield Before vs. After Conversion Drills

Flawed / Negative / Writer-Centered Draft Polished Reader-Centered ("You" Attitude) Revision
"We cannot refund your money because you failed to attach the original receipt." "Your refund can be processed immediately as soon as you provide the original receipt."
"You made several mistakes on your project timesheet, which caused our payroll to be delayed." "Please verify the highlighted hours on your project timesheet so we can disburse your salary promptly."
"We are closed on weekends, so don't call our customer service line until Monday." "Our customer support team is available to assist you Monday through Friday from 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM."
"I am writing this memo to tell you that I want all staff to submit their vacation requests by Friday." "To ensure your preferred holiday dates are approved, please submit your vacation schedules by Friday, November 5."
"Your claim is completely baseless because our software warranty does not cover user negligence." "While Section 8 of our service agreement outlines standard operating parameters, we would be pleased to assist you with system recalibration at our discounted loyalty rate."

2. Case Study: Bad Email Breakdown & Rewrite

Flawed Workplace Email
TO: all-engineering@fasttech.pk
FROM: bossman_rockz@fasttech.pk
SUBJECT: URGENT READ THIS NOW!!!!

Hey guys,
Some of you are being super lazy and leaving bug tickets open for weeks. It makes our department look terrible in front of the CEO. I need you to close all tickets ASAP. Do not complain to me about lack of time, just get it done. Also attached is the new company holiday list BTW.

Thx,
Hamza
Polished Professional Revision
TO: all-engineering@fasttech.pk
FROM: hamza.tariq@fasttech.pk
SUBJECT: Engineering Sprint Priority: Resolution of Outstanding Bug Tickets

Dear Engineering Team:

[ABSTRACT]
To ensure our upcoming Q4 software release meets client stability standards and our departmental performance metrics, we are focusing this week's sprint on resolving our outstanding bug tickets.

[BODY]
Currently, 34 customer-reported issue tickets remain pending in our Jira queue, with an average resolution latency exceeding our 7-day SLA. Prompt resolution ensures system reliability and reduces support escalation overhead for the entire department.
• Action Required: Please review your assigned queue in Jira by 2:00 PM today.
• Priority Triage: Focus on Level 1 and Level 2 critical blockers first.
• Support: If any ticket is blocked by third-party API dependencies, please flag it in our #dev-blockers Slack channel for immediate review.

[CONCLUSION]
Thank you for your dedicated effort in maintaining the stability of our production systems. Attached is our updated Q4 release schedule for your reference.

Best regards,

Hamza Tariq
Lead Engineering Manager | FastTech Solutions

Key Takeaways

  • Always eliminate accusatory words ("you failed", "your fault", "lazy").
  • Focus on reader benefits and solution steps instead of dwelling on past errors.
  • Use official usernames and clear, professional subject lines without exclamation marks.

3Document Analysis & Presentation Audits

Analyzing public brochures, letters, and presentation slide design errors

Document analysis tests your ability to evaluate real-world technical artifacts against the 6 characteristics of technical communication and Pfeiffer's design guidelines.

1. The 4-Step Document Evaluation Framework

Framework
  1. Identify Purpose: Is the document designed to inform, instruct, record, or persuade?
  2. Identify Audience: Who is the target reader? What is their knowledge level and operational need?
  3. Contrast with Academic Writing: Explain how the document differs from an academic paper (action-driven vs. grade-driven; visual hierarchy vs. dense prose).
  4. Assess Effectiveness: Evaluate clarity, conciseness, visual organization (headers, bullets, bolding), and adherence to the "You" attitude.

2. Presentation Audit Checklist: The Hashim Diagnostic

Presentation Dimension Hashim's Major Blunder Pfeiffer's Best Practice Fix
Color & Contrast Red text on blue background; arbitrary "fun" colors. High contrast: dark text on light, or light on dark. Never place red on blue (chromatic vibration). Max 3-4 colors.
Text Density Copied entire Wikipedia articles onto slides. Wall of text. Enforce the 6x6 rule: maximum 6 bullet points, maximum 6 words per line.
Typography Elaborate, decorative, curly font style. Clean sans-serif fonts (Calibri, Arial) at 36-44 pt for titles and 24-28 pt for bullets.
Delivery Method Attempted to memorize entire speech, then read slides verbatim. Deliver extemporaneously from bulleted cue cards. Memorize only the first 60 seconds.
Eye Contact & Posture Turned back to audience, looking up at the screen. Maintain continuous eye contact across the room; point with near hand.
Structure & Timing Jumped back and forth without roadmap; went overtime. Follow ABC format and Preacher's Maxim. Rehearse with a timer to finish under limit.
Q&A Readiness Panicked because he only knew the surface text on the slides. Research 5 times deeper than slide text. Follow 4-step Q&A method (Listen, Rephrase, Answer, Verify).

Key Takeaways

  • Always evaluate documents using Purpose, Audience, Academic differences, and Visual accessibility.
  • Presentations fail when slides become a teleprompter rather than visual support.

Simulation 2: Internal Investigation Memo
Prompt: Memo from Social Square Secretary to Customer Support Manager 
MEMORANDUM

DATE:    July 30, 2026
TO:      Mr. Adil Rizwan, Manager Customer Support
FROM:    Ms. Amna Ilyas, Secretary to Product Development Manager [AI]
SUBJECT: Technical Audit Report: Avanceon Private Limited On-Site Review

[ABSTRACT]
To follow up on the complaint letter received from Avanceon Private Limited, Mrs. Naveed (Product Development Manager) and our engineering team conducted an emergency on-site review at Avanceon's Gulberg offices on July 29, 2026. This memo summarizes our investigation findings and encloses the official field audit report for administrative action.

[BODY]
Our technical inspection confirmed that the software error was not caused by bugs in Social Square's source code. Rather, it was triggered by an external network security breach originating from an unauthorized computer terminal within Avanceon's local network:
• IP Intrusion Tracing: Multiple brute-force administrator password attempts originated from internal IP 192.168.10.45.
• Automated Lockdown: In accordance with our security architecture, our program locked the database to safeguard sensitive employee salary records from unauthorized manipulation.
• Data Integrity: All underlying employee payroll and bank account records remain 100% secure and uncorrupted.

[CONCLUSION]
Because the downtime resulted from an internal client security breach rather than vendor defect, we recommend the following steps:
1. Provide Avanceon's CEO with the enclosed forensic report so their network team can isolate the compromised terminal.
2. Issue an Adjustment Letter granting system reset codes once their internal network security is certified.

Enclosure: Field Visit Diagnostic & Security Report (Ref #AUD-2026-78)
cc: Mrs. Naveed, Manager Product Development

Key Takeaways

  • Ensure all 10 elements are included in formal letters.
  • Include Enclosure: and cc: notations whenever referenced in the prompt.
  • In memos, always omit salutation and complimentary close; include writer initials next to the FROM: line.

5Rapid Review Matrix & Formula Sheet

Essential summary tables and memory hooks for last-minute exam revision

1. Academic Writing vs. Workplace Writing

Feature Academic Writing Workplace Writing
Purpose Display student knowledge to earn a grade. Get work done and enable operational decisions.
Knowledge Gap Teacher knows more than student writer. Informed writer explains to uninformed reader.
Audience Single instructor (captive audience). Multiple busy stakeholders with diverse backgrounds.
Design Dense prose; few graphics. Scannable text, headers, bullets, tables, graphics.

3. Key Mnemonics & Formulas

The 3 Ps:

Prepare carefully, Practice often, Perform with enthusiasm.

The 6x6 Rule:

≤ 6 bullets per slide, ≤ 6 words per line.

The Preacher's Maxim:

Tell them what you will tell them → Tell them → Tell them what you told them.

Bad News Sequence:

Buffer → Reasons First → Diplomatic Refusal → Alternative → Goodwill.

4-Step Q&A:

Listen → Rephrase → Answer concisely → Verify satisfaction.

The 7 Cs:

Clear, Concise, Concrete, Correct, Coherent, Complete, Courteous.

Final Exam Advice

  • Manage your time: allocate time proportionally to question marks.
  • Always check for required end notations (Enclosure, cc) in document prompts.
  • Write clearly, state reasons before bad news, and embody the "You" attitude. Good luck!
